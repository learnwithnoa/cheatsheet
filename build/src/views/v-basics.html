Sentence basics

English is strict about word order. Spanish lets you move things around; English mostly doesn't.

In short
English always needs a subject and likes the order Subject + Verb + Object: It's raining · She reads a book.

Subject + verb + object

S She  V reads  O a book.

Siempre necesitas sujeto: It's raining · Is raining

Phrase, clause, sentence

Phrase

Words that work together, no subject + verb: on the table, very tired.

Clause

Has a subject + verb. She is tired (complete). Because she is tired… (needs more).

Sentence

A complete idea: Although it was raining, we went out.

Also good to know

Questions need a helper

Do you work here? · Did she call? · Are they tired? · Can I help?

En español basta la entonación. En inglés no.

Gerund or infinitive

Gerund = verb + -ing, used like a noun: I enjoy reading. Reading helps you learn.

Infinitive = to + base verb: I decided to read.

Which one you use depends on the verb that comes before.

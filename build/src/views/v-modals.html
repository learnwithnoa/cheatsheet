Modal verbs

Modal + base verb. No to, no -s, no -ed: she can swim · she cans swim · she can to swim

In short
Modal + base verb, with no to, -s or -ed: She can swim. can = ability · should = advice · must = obligation · might = maybe.
ModalUse it forExampleEspañol
canability, permission, requestsI can swim. · Can I open the window?poder, saber
couldpast ability, polite requests, possibility, suggestionsI could swim when I was seven. · Could you help me? · You could try again.podía, podría
be able toability in any tense (where can doesn't work)I'll be able to visit next year. · I've been able to rest.ser capaz de, poder
mayformal permission, possibilityMay I come in? · It may rain.puede que, ¿me permite…?
mightpossibility (less sure)I might go to the party.quizás, a lo mejor
muststrong obligation (often personal), deductionI must call my mom. · He must be tired.deber, tener que; debe de
have toobligation from outside (rules, work)I have to work on Saturday.tener que
don't have tonot necessary (you can if you want)You don't have to come.no es necesario
mustn'tprohibitionYou mustn't smoke here.no se debe, prohibido
shouldadvice, recommendation, expectationYou should see a doctor. · The bus should arrive soon.deberías
had betterstrong advice, a warningYou'd better leave now, or you'll miss the bus.más vale que
wouldhypothetical situations, polite offers and requestsI would travel more if I had time. · Would you like a coffee?-ría (viajaría)
willfuture, promises, quick decisionsI'll call you later.futuro (llamaré)
Careful: don't have to and mustn't are not the same. You don't have to pay = it's free. You mustn't pay = paying is not allowed.

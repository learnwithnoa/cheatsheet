Reported speech

How to tell someone what another person said. Usually the verb moves one step back into the past.

In short
Move the verb one step back: "I am tired" → She said she was tired. Say something · tell someone something.
direct

"I am tired," she said.

reported

She said (that) she was tired.

They saidYou reportExample
present simple: "I work"past simpleHe said he worked from home.
present continuous: "I'm leaving"past continuousShe said she was leaving.
past simple / present perfect: "I saw it" / "I've seen it"past perfectHe said he had seen it.
will: "I'll call"wouldShe said she would call.
can: "I can help"couldHe said he could help.
am going to: "I'm going to travel"was going toShe said she was going to travel.

Say or tell?

say + what: She said (that) she was late.

She said me

tell + person: She told me (that) she was late.

She told that

Questions, requests and time words

Questions: normal word order

"Where do you live?" → She asked me where I lived.
"Are you OK?" → He asked if I was OK.

Requests and orders

"Please wait." → He asked me to wait.
"Don't be late." → She told me not to be late.

Time and place words

today → that day · tomorrow → the next day · yesterday → the day before · now → then · here → there

If it's still true, you don't have to change the tense: She said she lives in Santiago (she still does). In conversation, this is very common.
